# Gameplay tuning notes

Skyfall uses an **arcade expert-control model**: roll banks the jet, pitch turns through the bank, and yaw is a slower independent correction. The player can complete rolls and loops. Inputs are applied in aircraft-local axes to a quaternion, avoiding the Euler pitch limit that previously snapped maneuvers. The published [Ace Combat expert-control description](https://en.bandainamcoent.eu/ace-combat/news/how-choose-your-difficulty-ace-combat-8-wings-of-theve) describes the same roll-then-pitch pattern. [NASA's flight-control overview](https://www.nasa.gov/stem-content/delta-wing-glider/) distinguishes the aileron, elevator, and rudder axes.

The speed model is deliberately slower than a real fighter for close city flight, but follows the energy tradeoff in the [FAA Airplane Flying Handbook, Chapter 4](https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/regulations_policies/handbooks_manuals/aviation/airplane_handbook/05_afh_ch4.pdf): climbs and hard turns consume speed; dives recover it. Acceleration is capped so afterburner does not instantly jump to top speed. The air brake slows the jet and increases turn authority. Low speed adds sink rather than an instant loss of control.

The Lyon collision field is rasterized from OpenStreetMap building footprints at 5 m spacing, with tagged or estimated building heights. The jet checks its center, nose, and wing tips against rooftop and ground heights, and periodically samples the loaded photomesh below its fuselage and nose. At a collision, the jet is placed above the surface before the crash is shown. The HUD shows AGL and warns before a descending terrain strike. The field remains an approximation outside loaded tiles and does not capture tunnels or bridges precisely.

Missiles can be fired without a target and then fly on their launch heading; an acquired lock gives them steering. The HUD projects a path and impact cue. Gravity bombs inherit aircraft velocity, fall under gravity, and have their own projected impact marker. The mission’s single nuclear bomb (unlimited in free flight) arms for 4.5 seconds on contact so the player can clear the area. Its blast is staged: flash, fast shock ring and delayed sound, expanding dust, and a slower rising cloud. Nearby targets and traffic are affected as the shock front reaches them. Tall structures break into sections; the inner city is cut to rubble height and outer upper floors are removed over several frames. Cannon rounds are simulated as fast projectiles, with surface, traffic, pedestrian, and mission-target hit checks. Missile and bomb impacts remove local patches of loaded photomesh and spawn falling pieces. Damage sites are reapplied to cached and newly streamed detail tiles.

Mission opponents now bank into pursuit turns and can shoot only while close and facing the player. A warning precedes each visible projectile, and the projectile must physically intersect the jet to inflict damage. This follows the readability principle in [Enemy Attacks and Telegraphing](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/enemy-attacks-and-telegraphing). The two interceptors enter from opposite sides so the player has to steer to acquire them; extraction also requires a turn. The current AI remains a simple pursuit behavior, not a full tactical dogfight system.
